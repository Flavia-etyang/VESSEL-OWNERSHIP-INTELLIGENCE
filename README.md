# VESSEL OWNERSHIP INTELLIGENCE

**Identify · Attribute · Reconstruct · Assess · Validate**

Transforming fragmented ownership, management and corporate information into structured maritime intelligence.

---

## Intelligence Question

### Who is connected to a vessel, in what capacity, during which period, and what does that relationship mean?

A vessel is rarely represented by a single **owner**.

Its corporate and operational structure can involve multiple entities with different legal, economic, technical, safety and commercial responsibilities.

The objective is to establish:

- Who is connected to the vessel
- What role each entity performs
- When the relationship was active
- Which relationships are directly supported by evidence
- What changes occurred over time
- What intelligence significance those relationships may have

> **Core principle:** Ownership intelligence is not a lookup exercise. It is the reconstruction and validation of relationships over time.

---

# 01 — Observation

The analysis begins with the available records.

The first question is:

> **What is directly documented?**

Observable information may include:

- Vessel name
- IMO number
- Flag
- Registered owner
- Beneficial owner
- Technical manager
- ISM company
- Operator
- Commercial manager
- Charterer
- Historical ownership records
- Corporate registration information
- Documented management changes

The IMO number is treated as the primary vessel reference when reconstructing relationships across changes in name, ownership or flag.

> **Analytical principle:** A documented record is evidence of a reported relationship. Its relevance and accuracy must still be assessed.

---

# 02 — Identification

Once relevant records have been collected, the next question is:

> **Which entities can be reliably identified?**

Vessel intelligence often involves fragmented information from multiple sources.

The analysis therefore seeks to resolve:

- Company names
- Alternative or historical company names
- Parent and subsidiary relationships
- Corporate jurisdictions
- Registered addresses
- Associated entities
- Vessel names and historical identities

A name match alone is not treated as sufficient identification.

```text
VESSEL
   ↓
ENTITY NAME
   ↓
ENTITY RESOLUTION
   ↓
CORROBORATING IDENTIFIERS
   ↓
ATTRIBUTABLE ENTITY
```

An entity must be resolved before a relationship is confidently assigned.

---

# 03 — Attribution

Each identified entity is then attributed to the role it performs in relation to the vessel.

## Ownership & Management Framework

A vessel is assessed as a network of relationships rather than a single ownership record.

```text
                         VESSEL
                           │
        ┌──────────────────┼──────────────────┐
        │                  │                  │
   OWNERSHIP          MANAGEMENT          COMMERCIAL
        │                  │                  │
 Beneficial Owner    Technical Manager     Charterer
 Registered Owner    ISM Company            Commercial Manager
                    Operator
```
---

## Seven-Role Attribution Framework

| Role | Primary Function | Intelligence Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Beneficial Owner** | Ultimately benefits from or exercises ownership or control interests | Helps establish ultimate economic interest and potential control exposure |
| **Registered Owner** | Legally recorded owner of the vessel | Establishes the formal ownership relationship |
| **Technical Manager** | Oversees technical operation, maintenance and vessel condition | Identifies responsibility for technical management |
| **ISM Company** | Responsible for safety-management obligations | Establishes the entity responsible for the vessel's safety-management system |
| **Operator** | Manages or controls aspects of the vessel's operational employment | Helps identify day-to-day operational responsibility |
| **Commercial Manager** | Oversees commercial employment or commercial management activities | Provides insight into commercial control and vessel employment |
| **Charterer** | Commercially employs the vessel under a charter arrangement | Provides context on the vessel's commercial use |

The same company may perform more than one role.

For example:
```text
ONE COMPANY
    ├── Registered Owner
    ├── Technical Manager
    └── ISM Company
```

Each role is nevertheless assessed independently.

> **Analytical principle: A company holding multiple roles does not make those roles interchangeable.**

---

# 04 — Reconstruction

Ownership intelligence is temporal.

The next question is:

When was each relationship active, and what changed?

The vessel's ownership and management structure is reconstructed across time.

```text
PAST
 │
 │  Owner A
 │  Manager A
 │  Flag A
 ▼
CHANGE EVENT
 ▼
PRESENT
 │
 │  Owner B
 │  Manager B
 │  Flag B
 ▼
ASSESSMENT
```

The analysis examines:

- Ownership transfers
- Changes in beneficial ownership
- Changes in registered ownership
- Management transitions
- Operator changes
- ISM changes
- Commercial changes
- Flag changes
- Continuity across relationships

The objective is not simply to identify that something changed.

The objective is to determine: 

> **What changed, when did it change, and which relationships remained continuous?**

---

# 05 — Assessment

The reconstructed relationship network is assessed for intelligence significance.

Depending on the case, the analysis may examine:

- Transparent ownership transitions
- Continuity or disruption in management
- Corporate restructuring
- Changes in vessel control
- Ownership opacity
- Relevant sanctions exposure
- Historical relationships
- Changes requiring further investigation

The assessment separates:

| Stage | Analytical Question |
|---|---|
| **Observation** | What relationship or change is documented? |
| **Identification** | Which entity is involved? |
| **Attribution** | What role does the entity perform? |
| **Reconstruction** | During which period was the relationship active? |
| **Assessment** | What does the relationship or change mean? |
| **Confidence** | How strongly is the assessment supported? |

> **Association alone is not treated as proof of responsibility, control or risk. The relationship, role and relevant time period must first be established.**

---

# 06 — Confidence

Confidence reflects the strength of the evidence supporting both:

1. The vessel–entity relationship
2. The resulting intelligence assessment

| Level | Intelligence Assessment |
|---|---|
| **HIGH** | The relationship is supported by multiple reliable and independent sources with consistent entity and temporal information. |
| **MEDIUM** | The relationship is plausible and supported by available evidence, but one or more elements remain incomplete, indirect or unresolved. |
| **LOW** | The available evidence is limited, conflicting or insufficient to establish the relationship or assessment reliably. |


## Confidence Factors

Confidence is assessed using factors including:

- Source reliability
- Source independence
- Entity-resolution certainty
- Consistency between records
- Temporal accuracy
- Corporate transparency
- Completeness of the ownership record
- Availability of corroborating information
- Directness of the vessel–entity relationship
- Presence of unresolved contradictions or information gaps

> **Confidence reflects the strength of the evidence and the quality of the relationship attribution — not the certainty of the analyst.**

---

# 07 — Intelligence Assessment

Each vessel is ultimately represented as a structured intelligence assessment:

```text
VESSEL
   ↓
DOCUMENTED RECORDS
   ↓
ENTITY IDENTIFICATION
   ↓
ROLE ATTRIBUTION
   ↓
TEMPORAL RECONSTRUCTION
   ↓
RELATIONSHIP ANALYSIS
   ↓
INTELLIGENCE ASSESSMENT
   ↓
CONFIDENCE
```

The final assessment distinguishes between:

**Documented relationships**
Relationships directly supported by available evidence.

**Attributed roles**
The specific capacity in which an identified entity is connected to the vessel.

**Historical relationships**
Relationships supported for a defined period but not necessarily current.

**Inference**
Analytical conclusions drawn from the available evidence.

**Unresolved gaps**
Relationships or information that cannot be established with sufficient
confidence.

**Confidence**
The strength of the evidence supporting the final assessment.

> **A vessel ownership assessment should establish relationships before assigning intelligence significance to them.**
